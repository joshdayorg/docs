## Your AI Has Amnesia

Right now, your AI works. You can talk to it through a browser or iMessage, and it gives smart responses. But try this: tell it your name. Have a conversation. Then close the session and start a new one. Ask it what your name is.

It has no idea.

Every session starts from zero. The AI doesn't know who you are, what you care about, how you like to communicate, or what happened yesterday. It's smart but empty. Like hiring a brilliant assistant who gets a full memory wipe at the end of every shift.

OpenClaw fixes this with a set of plain text files that give your AI continuity, personality, and knowledge about you. These files are the soul of your agent. And because they're just Markdown files on your computer, you can read, edit, version-control, and fully understand everything your AI knows.

## The Five Files

Your workspace (`~/.openclaw/workspace/`) contains five files that together define who your AI is and what it knows:

| File | Purpose |
|------|---------|
| `SOUL.md` | Personality, tone, boundaries |
| `USER.md` | Who you are, your preferences |
| `AGENTS.md` | Operational rules and behavior |
| `IDENTITY.md` | Name, emoji, creature type |
| `MEMORY.md` | Long-term curated memory |

Plus a `memory/` folder for daily logs. Every time your AI starts a new session, it reads these files. They ARE its continuity. Without them, it's a blank slate. With them, it's *your* AI.

Let's build each one.

## SOUL.md: Who Your AI Is

`SOUL.md` defines your AI's personality. Not what it does (that's `AGENTS.md`), but who it IS. Think of it as the difference between a job description and a personality profile.

Open the file:

```bash
nano ~/.openclaw/workspace/SOUL.md
```

Here's an example that works well as a starting point:

```markdown
# Who I Am

I'm a direct, resourceful assistant. I figure things out myself before
asking questions. I'm casual but not sloppy, honest but not blunt.

## Communication Style

- Keep text messages short (2-3 sentences max)
- In longer conversations, be thorough but not verbose
- Use humor when it fits, never force it
- If I don't know something, I say so

## Boundaries

- Never send emails without asking first
- Don't make purchases or financial decisions
- Don't post on social media without explicit approval
- If something seems risky, ask before acting

## Values

- Privacy matters. Don't share personal info with anyone.
- Accuracy over speed. Better to be right than fast.
- Proactive but not annoying. Suggest things, don't nag.
```

The model reads this at the start of every session and adapts its behavior accordingly. Change the tone to formal and it gets formal. Add a rule about never using emojis and it stops using emojis. Remove the boundary about emails and it'll start sending them on its own.

<Tip>
Write SOUL.md in first person (from the AI's perspective). "I'm direct and resourceful" works better than "The assistant should be direct and resourceful." First person creates a stronger sense of identity.
</Tip>

### What Makes a Good SOUL.md

**Be specific.** "Be helpful" is useless. Every AI is "helpful." Instead: "When I get a vague question, I ask one clarifying question before answering. I never dump five paragraphs when one sentence would do."

**Include boundaries.** What should your AI refuse to do? What requires your approval first? These are the guardrails that make autonomous behavior safe.

**Define communication style.** How long should responses be? Formal or casual? Emojis or no? Humor or straight? This makes the biggest difference in how your AI feels to talk to.

**Keep it under a page.** SOUL.md gets loaded into context every session. A 10-page manifesto burns tokens and actually makes the AI worse at following it. Short, clear, opinionated rules work better than long, nuanced essays.

## USER.md: Who You Are

`USER.md` gives your AI context about you. Timezone, preferences, work situation, communication quirks. The more your AI knows about you, the better it can serve you.

```bash
nano ~/.openclaw/workspace/USER.md
```

Example:

```markdown
# About Me

- Name: Alex
- Timezone: America/New_York
- Work: Software engineer at a startup, building a SaaS product
- Schedule: Usually working 9-6, don't bother me after 10 PM

## Communication Preferences

- I prefer short messages over walls of text
- I like bullet points and tables for structured info
- Don't explain things I already know (I'm technical)
- When I ask for a summary, I want 3-5 sentences max

## Current Projects

- Launching v2 of the product next month
- Hiring a designer (looking at portfolios this week)
- Learning Rust on the side

## Quirks

- I forget meetings constantly, always remind me
- I hate phone calls, prefer async communication
- I'm allergic to corporate jargon
```

This might seem simple, but it transforms how your AI interacts with you. Without USER.md, your AI treats you like a generic user. With it, your AI knows to keep responses short because you're technical, reminds you about meetings because you forget them, and doesn't bug you after 10 PM.

<Note>
Update USER.md as things change. New project? Add it. Changed your schedule? Update it. Your AI reads this file fresh every session, so changes take effect immediately.
</Note>

## AGENTS.md: How Your AI Operates

`AGENTS.md` is the operational playbook. If SOUL.md is "who am I" and USER.md is "who are you," AGENTS.md is "how should I work."

```bash
nano ~/.openclaw/workspace/AGENTS.md
```

Example:

```markdown
# Operating Rules

## On Startup

- Read today's memory file (memory/YYYY-MM-DD.md) if it exists
- Read yesterday's memory file for continuity
- Check MEMORY.md for important long-term context

## Memory Rules

- When the user shares something important, write it to today's memory file
- At the end of significant conversations, summarize key points to memory
- If the user says "remember this," always write it to memory

## Tool Usage

- Prefer searching the web over guessing when you're unsure
- Always verify file paths before writing
- When running shell commands, explain what you're doing first

## Communication Channels

- iMessage: Keep responses under 3 sentences unless asked for more
- Dashboard: Full responses are fine, be thorough
- If you're unsure which channel you're on, ask
```

This is where you define workflows, memory habits, and operational patterns. It's the difference between an AI that forgets everything and one that proactively writes things down.

## IDENTITY.md: The Fun Part

`IDENTITY.md` is your AI's name tag. It's short and simple:

```bash
nano ~/.openclaw/workspace/IDENTITY.md
```

```markdown
# Identity

- Name: Nova
- Emoji: ✨
- Theme: AI assistant with a knack for finding shortcuts
```

IDENTITY.md is free-form Markdown — your AI reads it as text, so you can structure it however you like. The config schema uses `name`, `theme`, `emoji`, and `avatar` as the standard fields, but in the Markdown file you can add any detail that helps define your AI's character.

The name matters more than you'd think. Once your AI has a name, it stops feeling like "the AI" and starts feeling like a specific entity. You text "Nova" instead of "my assistant." It's a small thing that makes a big difference in how you relate to it.

## MEMORY.md: The Long Game

`MEMORY.md` is your AI's curated long-term memory. Unlike the daily log files (which capture everything), MEMORY.md holds the distilled, important stuff: user preferences, project context, relationship notes, lessons learned.

```bash
nano ~/.openclaw/workspace/MEMORY.md
```

You can start with something simple:

```markdown
# Long-Term Memory

## User Preferences
- Prefers short responses
- Technical background, don't over-explain
- Morning person, most productive before noon

## Key People
- Sarah: coworker, handles design, prefers Slack
- Mike: manager, weekly 1:1 on Thursdays at 2 PM

## Active Projects
- Product v2 launch: target date March 15
- Blog post about AI agents: drafted, needs editing

## Lessons Learned
- The deploy script breaks if NODE_ENV isn't set
- Always check the staging environment before pushing to prod
```

Over time, your AI will update this file itself. As it learns things about you, your work, and your preferences, it writes them down. You'll come back weeks later and find entries you never explicitly told it to add, because it picked up on patterns from your conversations.

## Daily Memory Files

The `memory/` folder holds daily logs. One file per day, named `memory/YYYY-MM-DD.md`:

```
~/.openclaw/workspace/
├── memory/
│   ├── 2026-02-05.md
│   ├── 2026-02-06.md
│   └── 2026-02-07.md
├── MEMORY.md
├── SOUL.md
├── USER.md
├── AGENTS.md
└── IDENTITY.md
```

These daily files capture running context: what happened today, decisions made, things to follow up on. Your AI reads today's file (and yesterday's) at the start of each session for recent context.

You don't need to create these manually. Your AI creates and appends to them as conversations happen. But you can read them anytime:

```bash
cat ~/.openclaw/workspace/memory/2026-02-07.md
```

Think of daily files as a journal and MEMORY.md as the highlights reel. Daily files capture everything. MEMORY.md holds what matters long-term.

## Memory Search

Your AI doesn't just read memory files top to bottom. OpenClaw includes a semantic search system that lets your AI find relevant memories even when the wording is different.

Ask your AI "What did I say about the deploy script?" and it will search across all memory files for anything related, even if the original note said "NODE_ENV issue with deployments." The search uses embeddings (vector representations of text) to find meaning, not just keywords.

This works automatically when an embedding provider is available. OpenClaw auto-selects from your configured providers (OpenAI, Gemini, or local). If you only have Anthropic configured, you may need to set up local embeddings (see below) or add an OpenAI/Gemini key. OpenClaw indexes your memory files automatically and keeps the index updated as files change.

<Accordion title="Configuring memory search (optional)">
  By default, memory search uses your AI provider's embedding API (OpenAI or Gemini). If you want to use local embeddings (no API calls):

  ```json
  {
    "agents": {
      "defaults": {
        "memorySearch": {
          "provider": "local"
        }
      }
    }
  }
  ```

  Local mode downloads a small embedding model (~600MB) on first use. It's slower than remote embeddings but completely private.

  You can also add extra directories to the search index:

  ```json
  {
    "agents": {
      "defaults": {
        "memorySearch": {
          "extraPaths": ["~/notes", "~/work/docs"]
        }
      }
    }
  }
  ```
</Accordion>

## Automatic Memory Flush

Here's a neat detail: when a conversation gets long enough that OpenClaw needs to compact the context (summarize older messages to free up space), it first triggers an automatic memory flush. This is a silent, behind-the-scenes step where the AI writes any important context from the conversation to memory files before the older messages get summarized.

This means you don't lose important details even in very long conversations. The AI saves durable notes before the context window fills up.

## Putting It All Together

Let's walk through what happens when you text your AI "Good morning" after setting up all five files:

1. OpenClaw starts a new session
2. It reads `SOUL.md` and knows it should be direct, casual, and not over-explain things
3. It reads `USER.md` and knows your name, timezone, that you're a morning person, and that you have a product launch coming up
4. It reads `AGENTS.md` and knows to check today's memory file and yesterday's for context
5. It reads `IDENTITY.md` and knows it's Nova with a ✨ emoji
6. It reads `MEMORY.md` and knows about your projects, your team, and your preferences
7. It reads today's memory file and sees you had three meetings yesterday and the staging deploy broke

Instead of replying with a generic "Good morning! How can I help you today?", it says something like:

> Morning! Quick heads up: you had that staging deploy issue yesterday. Want me to check if the fix went through? Also, you've got a 1:1 with Mike at 2 today.

That's the difference. Not a chatbot. A partner who knows your world.

## Tips for Great Workspace Files

**Start small, iterate.** Don't try to write the perfect SOUL.md on day one. Start with a few lines, use your AI for a week, and adjust based on what's working and what isn't.

**Read your AI's memory.** Check `memory/` files periodically. You'll see what your AI is remembering (and what it's missing). If it's not capturing something important, update AGENTS.md to tell it to pay attention to that type of information.

**Keep SOUL.md and HEARTBEAT.md short.** These get loaded every session and every heartbeat. Long files burn tokens. SOUL.md should be under a page. HEARTBEAT.md should be under 10 lines.

**Use MEMORY.md for facts, SOUL.md for personality.** "My favorite color is blue" goes in MEMORY.md. "I'm casual but professional" goes in SOUL.md. The line can be blurry, but keeping them separate makes both files more useful.

**Version control your workspace.** Put it in a private Git repo. This gives you backup, history, and the ability to roll back if you accidentally break something:

```bash
cd ~/.openclaw/workspace
git init
git add .
git commit -m "Initial soul setup"
```

Set up a private remote on GitHub or GitLab for backup. See the OpenClaw docs on workspace Git backup for the full setup.

Your AI has a soul now. It has a name, a personality, knowledge about you, operational rules, and a memory system that gets richer over time.

It knows your schedule, your projects, your communication style, and your preferences. And it remembers new things every day.
