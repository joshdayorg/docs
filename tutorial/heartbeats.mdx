## The Missing Piece

You have an AI with a personality, memory, and knowledge about you. It knows your name, your projects, your schedule. That's already more than most people get from their AI tools.

But there's a problem: it only does things when you ask.

Text it "check my email" and it checks your email. Ask it "what's on my calendar" and it tells you. It's responsive, but it's not proactive. It waits for you like a waiter standing silently by your table until you wave them over.

What if your AI could wake up on its own every 30 minutes, scan your inbox, glance at your calendar, check for anything urgent, and only bother you when something actually needs your attention? And if nothing's going on, it just goes back to sleep. Silently.

That's what heartbeats do.

## What Is a Heartbeat?

A heartbeat is a periodic wake-up cycle. Every N minutes (30 by default), OpenClaw sends your AI a message that says, roughly, "Hey, wake up. Anything need attention?" The AI checks its HEARTBEAT.md file for a list of things to look at, runs through them, and either:

1. **Reports something important** and sends you a message, or
2. **Says "all clear"** and goes back to sleep with no notification

You don't hear from it unless there's something worth hearing. This is the fundamental difference between heartbeats and notifications. Notifications blast you every time something happens. Heartbeats filter through an intelligent layer first. Your AI decides whether something is worth interrupting you for.

Think of it like a consciousness loop. Every 30 minutes, your AI opens its eyes, looks around, decides if anything matters, and either acts or goes back to sleep.

## Your First Heartbeat

Good news: if you followed the installation guide, heartbeats are already running. The default interval is 30 minutes and it starts automatically with the gateway.

To confirm they're working, check your gateway status:

```bash
openclaw status
```

You should see heartbeat info in the output showing the interval and next scheduled beat.

The default heartbeat prompt tells your AI to read HEARTBEAT.md if it exists and follow it strictly. If nothing needs attention, it replies `HEARTBEAT_OK` and you never see a message. If something does need attention, the reply gets delivered to you.

## Writing HEARTBEAT.md

HEARTBEAT.md is a tiny checklist that tells your AI what to look at during each beat. Open it up:

```bash
nano ~/.openclaw/workspace/HEARTBEAT.md
```

Start simple:

```markdown
# Heartbeat Checklist

- Check if any recent messages need a follow-up
- If a background task finished, summarize the results
- If it's been quiet for 8+ hours during daytime, send a brief check-in
```

That's it. Three lines. Your AI reads this every 30 minutes, runs through the list, and handles whatever needs handling.

Want it to do more? Add more items:

```markdown
# Heartbeat Checklist

- Scan inbox for urgent emails (anything with "urgent", "ASAP", or from my boss)
- Check calendar for events in the next 2 hours
- If a background task finished, summarize the results
- Review any pending tasks I mentioned but haven't completed
- If it's been quiet for 8+ hours during daytime, do a brief check-in
```

Every line you add is another thing your AI checks on each beat. But there's an important rule: **keep this file short**.

<Warning>
HEARTBEAT.md gets loaded into the AI's context every single heartbeat. A 50-line manifesto burns tokens every 30 minutes. Keep it under 10 lines. If you need your AI to do complex periodic work, that's what cron jobs are for (covered in a later module).
</Warning>

### What Makes a Good HEARTBEAT.md

**Be specific about triggers.** "Check email" is vague. "Check email for anything from my boss or with 'urgent' in the subject" gives the AI clear criteria for what matters.

**Include conditions.** "If it's been quiet for 8+ hours during daytime, send a check-in" is better than "Check in periodically." The condition prevents your AI from pinging you every 30 minutes with "Hey, need anything?"

**Focus on monitoring, not heavy work.** Heartbeats are for quick checks and awareness. "Scan inbox for urgent items" is good. "Write a comprehensive weekly report analyzing all project metrics" is too heavy for a heartbeat. That's a cron job.

**Leave out things that happen on their own.** Don't put "respond to incoming messages" in HEARTBEAT.md. Your AI already does that when a message arrives. Heartbeats are for things that don't have their own trigger.

### The Empty File Trick

Here's a useful detail: if HEARTBEAT.md exists but is effectively empty (just blank lines or headers with no actual content), OpenClaw skips the heartbeat entirely. No API call, no tokens burned. This is an easy way to pause heartbeats without changing your config:

```markdown
# Heartbeat Checklist

<!-- Nothing to check right now -->
```

When you want heartbeats active again, just add your checklist items back.

## Configuring the Interval

The default heartbeat interval is 30 minutes. You can change it in your OpenClaw config:

```bash
nano ~/.openclaw/openclaw.json
```

Find (or add) the heartbeat section under `agents.defaults`:

```json
{
  "agents": {
    "defaults": {
      "heartbeat": {
        "every": "30m"
      }
    }
  }
}
```

Change `"30m"` to whatever interval you want:

- `"15m"` for more frequent checks (burns more tokens)
- `"1h"` for hourly checks (saves tokens)
- `"2h"` for light monitoring
- `"0m"` to disable heartbeats completely

After changing the config, reload OpenClaw:

```bash
kill -USR1 $(pgrep -f "openclaw gateway")
```

Or just restart the gateway:

```bash
openclaw gateway restart
```

<Tip>
Start with 30 minutes. That's frequent enough to catch most things without running up your API bill. If you find your AI is surfacing stale information (stuff that was already relevant 2 hours ago), drop it to 15 minutes. If you're paying too much and don't need fast responses, go to 1 hour.
</Tip>

## Quiet Hours: Respecting Your Schedule

Nobody wants their AI texting them at 3 AM about a non-urgent email. Active hours let you restrict heartbeats to specific times of day.

Add an `activeHours` block to your heartbeat config:

```json
{
  "agents": {
    "defaults": {
      "heartbeat": {
        "every": "30m",
        "activeHours": {
          "start": "08:00",
          "end": "22:00"
        }
      }
    }
  }
}
```

With this config, heartbeats only run between 8 AM and 10 PM. Outside that window, they're completely silent. No API calls, no token usage, no messages.

The timezone defaults to whatever you set as your `userTimezone` in the agent config. You can also set it explicitly:

```json
{
  "activeHours": {
    "start": "09:00",
    "end": "22:00",
    "timezone": "America/New_York"
  }
}
```

<Tip>
Match active hours to your actual schedule, not when you're theoretically available. If you usually check your phone at 7 AM but your first meeting is at 9, setting start to `"07:00"` means you'll get a heads-up about your 9 AM meeting while you're having coffee. That's the sweet spot.
</Tip>

## Where Heartbeat Messages Go

When your AI finds something worth reporting, it needs somewhere to send the message. The `target` setting controls this:

```json
{
  "agents": {
    "defaults": {
      "heartbeat": {
        "every": "30m",
        "target": "last"
      }
    }
  }
}
```

**`"last"` (default):** Sends to whatever channel you last used. If your last conversation was through iMessage, heartbeat alerts go to iMessage. If it was through the web dashboard, they go there.

**A specific channel:** Set it to `"whatsapp"`, `"telegram"`, `"imessage"`, `"discord"`, or `"slack"` to always deliver to that channel.

**`"none"`:** The heartbeat runs (AI checks things, can update memory, run tools) but no message is sent to you. Useful for background processing where the AI maintains awareness without needing to report.

If you want heartbeat messages to go to a specific person or chat, add a `to` field:

```json
{
  "heartbeat": {
    "every": "30m",
    "target": "whatsapp",
    "to": "+15551234567"
  }
}
```

## The HEARTBEAT_OK Contract

When your AI checks everything and nothing needs attention, it replies with exactly `HEARTBEAT_OK`. This is a special keyword that OpenClaw recognizes.

Here's what happens with `HEARTBEAT_OK`:

1. The reply is suppressed. You never see it. No "all clear!" notification every 30 minutes.
2. No message is sent to any channel.
3. The heartbeat is logged internally so you can verify it ran.

If the AI's reply starts or ends with `HEARTBEAT_OK` and the rest is under 300 characters, the whole thing gets dropped as an acknowledgment. This means the AI can include a brief internal note (for logging purposes) without it getting delivered to you.

If the AI finds something important, it just sends a normal reply. No `HEARTBEAT_OK` anywhere. That reply goes to whatever channel `target` is set to.

<Note>
You can adjust how many extra characters are allowed alongside `HEARTBEAT_OK` with the `ackMaxChars` setting. The default is 300. If your AI tends to add chatty "all clear" messages, lower this number.
</Note>

## Visibility Controls

Sometimes you want more (or less) visibility into what your heartbeats are doing. OpenClaw gives you per-channel control:

```json
{
  "channels": {
    "defaults": {
      "heartbeat": {
        "showOk": false,
        "showAlerts": true,
        "useIndicator": true
      }
    }
  }
}
```

**`showOk`:** Should `HEARTBEAT_OK` acknowledgments be shown? Default is `false` (silent). Set to `true` on a specific channel if you want confirmation that heartbeats are running.

**`showAlerts`:** Should alert messages be delivered? Default is `true`. Set to `false` to suppress heartbeat alerts on a specific channel (useful for noisy channels).

**`useIndicator`:** Emit status indicator events for dashboards that show heartbeat status. Default is `true`.

You can override these per channel:

```json
{
  "channels": {
    "defaults": {
      "heartbeat": {
        "showOk": false,
        "showAlerts": true
      }
    },
    "telegram": {
      "heartbeat": {
        "showOk": true
      }
    }
  }
}
```

This shows `HEARTBEAT_OK` confirmations on Telegram (so you can see the pulse) while keeping other channels silent.

## A Real Heartbeat in Action

Let's put it all together. Here's a complete setup for an AI that monitors your world every 30 minutes during waking hours:

**In `~/.openclaw/openclaw.json`:**

```json
{
  "agents": {
    "defaults": {
      "heartbeat": {
        "every": "30m",
        "target": "last",
        "activeHours": {
          "start": "07:00",
          "end": "23:00"
        }
      }
    }
  }
}
```

**In `~/.openclaw/workspace/HEARTBEAT.md`:**

```markdown
# Heartbeat Checklist

- Check for urgent emails (from my boss, tagged urgent, or unread for 2+ hours)
- Scan calendar for events in the next 2 hours and remind me if needed
- If a background task or sub-agent finished work, summarize what happened
- If nothing has happened for 8+ hours during daytime, brief friendly check-in
```

**What happens at 9:30 AM:**

Your AI wakes up, reads the checklist, and scans your email. It finds a message from your boss marked urgent that arrived 45 minutes ago. It also notices you have a meeting in 90 minutes that you haven't prepared for. It sends you:

> You got an urgent email from Sarah about the Q3 budget. She needs your numbers by noon. Also, you have the product review at 11 AM. Want me to pull together the latest metrics?

**What happens at 10:00 AM:**

Your AI wakes up again. The urgent email situation is being handled (you responded), no new emails need attention, the 11 AM meeting is now only an hour away but you already know about it. Nothing actionable.

The AI replies `HEARTBEAT_OK`. You hear nothing. It goes back to sleep.

That's the rhythm. Wake, check, act or sleep. Every 30 minutes. Only bothering you when there's a reason to.

## Heartbeat vs Cron: The Key Insight

OpenClaw has two scheduling systems, and understanding the difference matters:

**Heartbeats are a consciousness loop.** Your AI wakes up, surveys its world, and makes judgment calls about what's important. It batches multiple checks into one turn. It has full context from your conversations. It's smart about what to surface.

**Cron jobs are calendar appointments.** They fire at exact times for specific tasks. "Generate a daily report at 7 AM." "Remind me about the meeting in 20 minutes." "Run a deep analysis every Monday at 9 AM." No judgment, just execution.

Here's the practical difference:

| | Heartbeat | Cron Job |
|---|---|---|
| **When it runs** | Every N minutes (approximate) | At exact times |
| **What it does** | Checks a list, decides what matters | Runs one specific task |
| **Context** | Full conversation history | Fresh start (isolated) or injected event (main) |
| **Best for** | Monitoring, awareness, quick checks | Reports, reminders, scheduled deliverables |
| **Token cost** | One turn handles all checks | One turn per job |

The most efficient setup uses both. Heartbeats handle the "periodic awareness" stuff: email scanning, calendar monitoring, background task status. Cron jobs handle the "do this specific thing at this specific time" stuff: morning briefings, weekly reviews, one-shot reminders.

<Accordion title="Quick preview: what cron jobs look like">
We'll cover cron jobs in detail in a later module, but here's a taste:

```bash
# Morning briefing at 7 AM every day
openclaw cron add \
  --name "Morning briefing" \
  --cron "0 7 * * *" \
  --tz "America/Los_Angeles" \
  --session isolated \
  --message "Generate today's briefing: weather, calendar summary, top emails." \
  --announce

# One-shot reminder in 20 minutes
openclaw cron add \
  --name "Meeting reminder" \
  --at "20m" \
  --session main \
  --system-event "Reminder: standup starts in 10 minutes." \
  --wake now \
  --delete-after-run
```

The key difference: cron jobs can run in **isolated sessions** (clean slate, no conversation context, optionally with a different model) and they fire at **exact times**. Heartbeats always run in your main session with full context.
</Accordion>

## Manual Wake: On-Demand Heartbeat

Sometimes you don't want to wait for the next heartbeat cycle. Maybe you just got a bunch of emails and want your AI to scan them now. You can trigger an immediate heartbeat with:

```bash
openclaw system event --text "Check for urgent follow-ups" --mode now
```

This enqueues a system event and triggers your AI to wake up immediately, running through its normal heartbeat checklist plus handling whatever text you included.

## Cost Awareness

Heartbeats aren't free. Every beat is a full agent turn, which means API tokens. Here's a rough sense of the cost picture:

- **30-minute intervals, 16 active hours a day** = about 32 heartbeat turns per day
- **Each turn with a short HEARTBEAT.md** = relatively cheap (small prompt, short response)
- **Each turn with a 50-line HEARTBEAT.md that triggers email scanning, calendar checks, and web searches** = more expensive

The biggest cost lever is the size of your HEARTBEAT.md and what tools your AI uses during each beat. A checklist that says "scan inbox" triggers tool calls to read your email, which adds tokens. A checklist that just says "check if any tasks are pending" might not trigger any tools at all.

Tips for managing cost:

- **Keep HEARTBEAT.md tiny.** Every line is checked every beat.
- **Use `activeHours`.** Don't run heartbeats while you're sleeping.
- **Use a longer interval if you don't need fast response.** `"1h"` cuts your cost in half compared to `"30m"`.
- **Use `target: "none"` for internal-only processing.** If you just want the AI to maintain awareness without ever messaging you, this saves the delivery step.
- **Move heavy work to cron.** A daily email summary as a cron job costs one turn per day. The same thing in a heartbeat costs one turn per beat.

<Accordion title="Using a cheaper model for heartbeats (optional)">
If your main sessions use an expensive model like Opus but your heartbeat checks are simple, you can override the model for heartbeat runs only:

```json
{
  "agents": {
    "defaults": {
      "heartbeat": {
        "every": "30m",
        "model": "anthropic/claude-sonnet-4-20250514"
      }
    }
  }
}
```

This runs heartbeats on Sonnet (cheaper and faster) while keeping your interactive conversations on whatever model you normally use. The tradeoff is that Sonnet might miss nuance that Opus would catch, but for simple "is anything urgent?" checks, it works great.
</Accordion>

## Per-Agent Heartbeats

If you're running multiple agents (covered in a later module), each agent can have its own heartbeat config. This is defined in the `agents.list` section:

```json
{
  "agents": {
    "defaults": {
      "heartbeat": {
        "every": "30m",
        "target": "last"
      }
    },
    "list": [
      {
        "id": "main",
        "default": true
      },
      {
        "id": "ops",
        "heartbeat": {
          "every": "1h",
          "target": "slack",
          "to": "channel:C1234567890"
        }
      }
    ]
  }
}
```

Important detail: if ANY agent in `agents.list` has a `heartbeat` block, **only agents with an explicit heartbeat block will run heartbeats.** In the example above, only the "ops" agent gets heartbeats. The "main" agent doesn't, even though `agents.defaults.heartbeat` is configured. This is intentional, to prevent every agent from beating when you only want specific ones to.

If you want the main agent to also have heartbeats when other agents have them, give it an explicit heartbeat block too (even an empty one that inherits from defaults):

```json
{
  "list": [
    {
      "id": "main",
      "default": true,
      "heartbeat": {}
    },
    {
      "id": "ops",
      "heartbeat": {
        "every": "1h",
        "target": "slack"
      }
    }
  ]
}
```

Don't worry about this too much if you're running a single agent. It only matters when you add more agents later.

## Debugging Heartbeats

If heartbeats don't seem to be working, here are the common issues:

<Accordion title="Heartbeats not firing at all">
**Check the gateway is running:**
```bash
openclaw status
```
Heartbeats run inside the gateway process. If the gateway isn't running, nothing fires.

**Check the interval isn't disabled:**
Make sure `heartbeat.every` isn't set to `"0m"` in your config.

**Check active hours:**
If `activeHours` is configured, heartbeats only run inside that window. If it's 2 AM and your active hours start at 8 AM, that's expected.

**Check HEARTBEAT.md isn't empty:**
If the file exists but has only blank lines or header-only content, OpenClaw skips the beat to save API calls. Add at least one real checklist item.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Getting HEARTBEAT_OK but no real alerts">
Your AI is running but deciding nothing needs attention. This could mean:

- **Your checklist is too vague.** "Check for important stuff" gives the AI too much room to decide nothing is important. Be specific: "Check email for messages from Sarah or Mike."
- **The AI doesn't have the right tools.** If your checklist says "check email" but the email skill isn't installed, the AI can't actually check email. It might just say everything's fine.
- **Nothing actually needs attention.** Sometimes all clear really means all clear!

Try setting `showOk: true` on one channel temporarily to confirm beats are running:
```json
{
  "channels": {
    "telegram": {
      "heartbeat": {
        "showOk": true
      }
    }
  }
}
```
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Getting too many heartbeat messages">
Your AI is being too eager about surfacing things. Fix this by:

- **Adding conditions to your checklist.** Change "Check email" to "Check email only for urgent items from my boss."
- **Increasing the interval.** Go from `"30m"` to `"1h"` to get half as many beats.
- **Making boundaries explicit in SOUL.md.** Add something like "Only send heartbeat alerts for things that genuinely need my attention in the next hour. If it can wait, let it wait."
</Accordion>
